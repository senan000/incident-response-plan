# Developing an Incident Response Plan

## 1. Introduction

### Background

The organization has detected unusual activity that may indicate unauthorized access to sensitive information. A structured incident response process is required to contain the incident, protect affected systems and reduce potential damage.

### Purpose and Scope

The purpose of this plan is to provide a structured approach for detecting, analyzing, containing, eradicating and recovering from cybersecurity incidents.

The plan applies to employees, information systems, networks, applications and sensitive organizational data.

---

## 2. Incident Response Process

The incident response process follows the main phases described in NIST SP 800-61.

### 2.1 Preparation

Preparation ensures that the organization is ready to respond to security incidents.

Recommended actions:

- Create an incident response team.
- Define roles and responsibilities.
- Maintain updated contact information.
- Prepare incident response procedures.
- Configure logging and monitoring.
- Maintain secure backups.
- Conduct regular security awareness training.
- Test the incident response plan.

### 2.2 Detection and Analysis

The organization identifies and analyzes suspicious activity.

Recommended actions:

- Review security logs and alerts.
- Identify affected systems and accounts.
- Determine the source and scope of the incident.
- Preserve relevant evidence.
- Assess the potential impact.
- Classify the incident based on severity.

### 2.3 Containment

The objective is to prevent the incident from spreading.

Recommended actions:

- Isolate compromised systems.
- Disable compromised accounts.
- Block malicious network connections.
- Apply temporary access restrictions.
- Preserve evidence before making major changes.

### 2.4 Eradication and Recovery

After containment, the organization removes the cause of the incident and restores normal operations.

Recommended actions:

- Remove malware or malicious files.
- Patch exploited vulnerabilities.
- Reset compromised credentials.
- Restore systems from trusted backups.
- Monitor restored systems.
- Confirm that the threat has been removed.

### 2.5 Post-Incident Activity

After recovery, the organization reviews the incident.

Recommended actions:

- Document the incident timeline.
- Identify the root cause.
- Review the effectiveness of the response.
- Record lessons learned.
- Update policies and procedures.
- Provide additional employee training.
- Improve security controls.

---

## 3. Team Roles and Responsibilities

### Incident Commander

- Coordinates the overall response.
- Makes major response decisions.
- Assigns tasks to team members.
- Ensures communication between teams.

### Technical Specialist

- Investigates affected systems.
- Analyzes logs and technical evidence.
- Performs containment and eradication.
- Assists with system recovery.

### Communications Lead

- Coordinates internal communication.
- Provides approved updates to management.
- Handles communication with relevant stakeholders.
- Ensures that sensitive information is not disclosed improperly.

### Security/Forensics Specialist

- Collects and preserves evidence.
- Performs forensic analysis.
- Documents indicators of compromise.
- Supports root-cause analysis.

Responsibilities can be transferred to another qualified team member if the assigned person becomes unavailable.

---

## 4. Post-Incident Analysis and Lessons Learned

A post-incident review should be performed after the incident is resolved.

The review should include:

- What happened?
- When was the incident detected?
- How did the attacker gain access?
- Which systems and data were affected?
- Which response actions were successful?
- What could have been improved?

The organization should document the findings and create corrective actions.

Lessons learned should be used to:

- Update incident response procedures.
- Improve security monitoring.
- Strengthen access controls.
- Improve employee training.
- Patch identified vulnerabilities.
- Test the updated response plan regularly.

---

## 5. Conclusion

A well-structured incident response plan helps an organization respond quickly and effectively to cybersecurity incidents. Following NIST SP 800-61 provides a consistent approach for preparation, detection, containment, eradication, recovery and post-incident improvement.

The plan should be tested and updated regularly to address new threats and changes in the organization's environment.
